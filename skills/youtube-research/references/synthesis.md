# Writing up YouTube research

## Structure

1. **Answer first**: 3–5 bullet findings that answer the user's question, each with counts ("7 of 19 hooks are result-first").
2. **The table**: one row per item with verbatim text and a `?t=` link. Sort by what the user cares about (usually views/day or ×median, not raw views).
3. **Patterns / taxonomy**: group items by type; 1–2 best examples per group, linked.
4. **Coverage**: queries and languages run, unique videos found, videos reviewed, skipped and why. One or two lines.

## Rules

- Every factual sentence about a video links to the moment that supports it.
- Quotes are verbatim from tool output; paraphrases are clearly not in quotation marks.
- Numbers come from tool output (views, dates, ×median), not from memory.
- Say what the data cannot show: a high-view video's hook is correlated with success, not proven to cause it; auto-captions may contain transcription errors; the sample is YouTube's search ranking, not all videos on the topic.
- Keep the user's language for the write-up (e.g. German), but keep quotes in their original language and add a translation in brackets when the user may not read it.
